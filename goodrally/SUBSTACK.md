## 2. Announcement post

**Title:** I'm starting a club for people who've never found a reason to use AI
**Subtitle:** Good Rally: 30 days, one short message a day, all about your health

---

> ✏️ **Open with your own true story.** The draft below is a placeholder, so replace it with a real moment: a parent, a friend, a reader who asked you what AI is actually for.

[Someone close to me] asked me recently what "all this AI stuff" was actually for.

Not in a dismissive way. They were curious. They'd seen the headlines and quietly assumed it wasn't for them. Too techy. Too much to learn. Probably a bit risky.

I realised I didn't have a good answer. Not because there isn't one, but because nobody had shown them a reason to try it that mattered to *them*.

So here's the reason I'd give now: **your health.**

Not diagnosing anything. Not replacing your doctor. Just the everyday, fiddly, slightly overwhelming parts of looking after yourself:

- Getting ready for an appointment so you don't forget the one question you meant to ask.
- Making sense of a letter from your insurer.
- Turning what's in the fridge into a dinner that fits your blood pressure.
- A gentle ten-minute routine for a stiff back, built around *your* body.
- Spotting the scams that now use AI to sound like someone you love.

That's what **Good Rally** is.

**How it works:** for 30 days, you get one short message each morning, by text, email or WhatsApp. Each one shows you a single useful thing to try, with the exact words to type or say. It takes about five minutes. If you get stuck, you reply and a real person (me, for now) helps.

Along the way you learn the habits that matter more than any trick: how to double-check what AI tells you, what never to share, and when to stop asking a chatbot and call your doctor. AI is your helper, never your doctor. That rule is in every single day.

**Why "Good Rally"?** To rally is to recover your strength. And a good rally in tennis is a back-and-forth, which is exactly how you should use AI: you ask, it answers, you question it, you check.

**Who it's for:** anyone who's curious but hasn't found a way in. It's written especially for people in their 50s, 60s and 70s, with big type, plain words and no jargon. And if that's not you, it might be your mum or dad. You can sign a parent up, or do the 30 days together.

**The founding group:** I'm opening it to a small group first. It's $29 for the 30 days, and if it isn't for you by day 7, you get a full refund.

**If you're a paid subscriber to the perennial report, Good Rally is included.** Your code is on its way in a separate email. Thank you for supporting this newsletter; this is one way I can say it back.

👉 **[Save your spot at goodrally.club](https://goodrally.club)**

Good Rally is its own thing, but it grew out of everything we talk about here: moving well, recovering well, and taking care of ourselves for the long run. This newsletter stays exactly as it is, and free.

If you have a question about it, hit reply. I read everything.

**Wed · A sample day**
> A Good Rally sample: before your next doctor's visit, open any AI app and say *"I have an appointment about my knee on Thursday. Help me write down when it started, what makes it worse, and 5 questions to ask."* Five minutes. You walk in organised. That's one day of thirty.

**Thu · The safety rule**
> The rule behind Good Rally: AI is your helper, never your doctor. Every day of the 30 teaches one useful thing *and* one habit: double-check it, don't overshare, know when to call a professional. That second part matters more than any prompt.
