# Good Rally: the 30-day challenge

One message a day, sent at 8:00 am in the member's chosen channel (text, email or WhatsApp). Each is written to be read in under a minute and tried in about five.

**House rules for every message**
- One idea per day. Always give the exact words to type or say, in *italics*.
- End with a reply hook: *Reply DONE when you've tried it, or HELP if you get stuck.* A real person answers HELP within the day.
- Warm, plain words. No jargon, no emoji walls, no exclamation-mark overload.
- Health safety: AI helps you **prepare, organise and understand**. It never diagnoses, never changes medication, and never replaces a professional. Days with health information carry a one-line reminder.
- Privacy: never ask members to share records with us, and teach what not to paste into AI (full name + date of birth together, insurance or Social Security numbers, account logins).

Placeholders: `{name}` is the member's first name.

## Week 1: Getting comfortable

### Day 1 · Say hello
Good morning, {name}, and welcome to Good Rally. For the next 30 days you'll get one small, useful thing to try. Today is just about getting set up.

On your phone, download one free AI app: **ChatGPT**, **Claude** or **Gemini**. Any of them is fine. Open it and type (or tap the microphone and say):

*"Hi! I'm new to this. In simple words, what kinds of things can you help me with day to day?"*

That's it. You've started.
Reply DONE when you've tried it, or HELP if you get stuck with the download. We'll walk you through it.

### Day 2 · Just talk
Typing on a phone is fiddly. Good news: you don't have to. In your AI app, look for the **microphone** or **headphones** button, tap it, and just speak.

Try saying: *"I'd like to feel more energetic in the afternoons. Can you ask me a few questions about my day first?"*

Answer its questions out loud, like a conversation. Notice how it asks before it advises. That's a good sign.
Reply DONE, or HELP if you can't find the microphone.

### Day 3 · Plain English, please
Ever left an appointment with a word you didn't understand? Today, pick a health word you've heard but never quite got, such as "cholesterol", "A1C", "arthritis" or "blood pressure numbers", and ask:

*"Explain [the word] to me like I'm a smart friend who isn't a doctor. Keep it short, and tell me what question I could ask my doctor about it."*

If the answer is still too complicated, say *"Simpler, please."* It won't mind.
Reminder: AI explains; your doctor decides. Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 4 · How to double-check it
AI is helpful, but it can be confidently wrong. Today you'll learn the most important habit in this whole club: **checking**.

Ask any health question you're curious about, then follow up with:

*"How sure are you about that? What would make you wrong, and where could I check it — like a hospital or government health website?"*

Good answers admit uncertainty. If it can't point you somewhere trustworthy, treat the answer as a starting point, not the truth.
Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 5 · What never to share
Today's a short, important one. When you use AI, **never** type in:
- your full name together with your date of birth
- your Social Security, Medicare or insurance numbers
- passwords, bank details, or photos of your ID or cards

You *can* describe your situation in general terms: "a woman in her 60s with a sore knee" works just as well as your name.

Try it: *"I'm in my [age range] and I have [a general issue]. What are some good questions to ask my doctor?"*
Reply DONE, or reply with any privacy question. We're happy to help.

### Day 6 · A walking companion
Today, take AI for a walk (or a sit in a nice chair). Open voice mode and say:

*"I'm going for a 15-minute walk. Keep me company. Ask me an interesting question about my life, and after my answer, tell me something fascinating about nature I might see today."*

Some members use this every day. It's a lovely way to make a walk go by.
Reply DONE and tell us what it asked you.

### Day 7 · Your first week, done
One week in, {name}. That's a real habit forming. Today's task is a gentle one:

*"This week I learned to use you for [one thing you liked]. Based on that, suggest three small ways I could use you for my health next week."*

Save anything you like: most apps let you scroll back through old chats.
Reply with the one thing you've liked most so far. We read every reply.

## Week 2: Your care, organised

### Day 8 · Before your appointment
Got a doctor's visit coming up, or one you've been putting off? AI is wonderful at getting you ready. Try:

*"I have an appointment about [the issue]. Help me write down when it started, what makes it better or worse, and the five most useful questions to ask. Keep it to one page."*

Take the list with you, or read it off your phone. Doctors appreciate an organised patient.
Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 9 · Your medication list
Keeping track of medications is hard, especially when they change. Today, make a clean list, **without** sharing your name or personal details:

*"Help me make a simple table of my medications with columns for: name, what it's for, how much, and when I take it. I'll tell you them one at a time."*

Then ask: *"What questions could I ask my pharmacist about taking these together?"*
Important: never start, stop or change a medication based on AI. Take your questions to your pharmacist or doctor. Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 10 · Make sense of a bill
Medical bills and insurance letters can be baffling. Pick one (cover or blank out your name, member ID and account numbers first) and describe it or type the confusing part:

*"I got an insurance letter that says [copy the confusing sentence]. What does this mean in plain English, and what should I ask when I call them?"*

It won't fix the bill, but it can help you make a calmer, clearer phone call.
Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 11 · Your after-visit notes, decoded
After an appointment, you often get a summary full of shorthand. Choose a line or two that confused you (leave out your name) and ask:

*"My visit summary says: '[the line]'. Explain what it means, and tell me if there's anything I might want to follow up on."*

If anything sounds worrying, that's a reason to call your doctor's office, not to worry alone.
Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 12 · A symptom diary
Doctors love patterns: when something happens, how long, how bad. Today, set up a simple diary:

*"I want to keep track of [e.g. headaches, sleep, knee pain] for two weeks. Make me a very simple daily check-in with three questions I can answer in under a minute."*

Then each evening, answer the three questions in the same chat. You'll have a clear record for your next visit.
Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 13 · Caring for someone else
Many of us help a parent, partner or friend with their health. AI can take some of the load. Try:

*"I help look after my [relationship], who [general situation]. Help me make a weekly checklist of things to keep on top of, and suggest a kind way to talk to them about [a tricky topic]."*

Caring is tiring. It's okay to ask for help, from people and from tools.
Reply DONE, or tell us who you're caring for if you'd like ideas.

### Day 14 · Two weeks strong
Halfway, {name}. Let's look back:

*"Over the last two weeks I've used you to [list two or three things]. Which of these is most worth turning into a weekly habit, and how could I remember to do it?"*

Reply with your answer. And if a friend might enjoy this, forward them this message: *goodrally.club*.

## Week 3: Everyday habits

### Day 15 · Dinner from your fridge
Open your fridge, take a photo with your AI app (look for the camera or paperclip button) and ask:

*"Here's what's in my fridge. Suggest three simple dinners I could make tonight that are [heart-healthy / low in salt / easy on my stomach]. Keep the steps short."*

No photo? Just list what you have. It works just as well.
Reply DONE and tell us what you made.

### Day 16 · Gentle movement that fits your body
Today, a movement routine made for *you*:

*"Make me a gentle 10-minute routine I can do at home. I'm [age range], I have [any limits, e.g. a bad knee, balance worries], and I'd like to [goal, e.g. feel less stiff]. Include seated options."*

Start slow. If anything hurts, stop. And if you have a condition, share the routine with your doctor or physical therapist before you begin.
Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 17 · A calmer evening
Sleep and stress go hand in hand. Tonight, try:

*"Help me design a calm 20-minute wind-down routine for bedtime. I usually [describe your evening]. Keep it realistic, and give me a short breathing exercise to finish."*

Want more? Ask it to *"read me a slow, calming story about a walk by the sea"* in voice mode as you settle in.
Reply DONE tomorrow and tell us how you slept.

### Day 18 · Remember the little things
Water, stretching, pills, calling your sister. Little things slip. Ask:

*"I want to remember to [drink more water / stretch / take my evening tablets]. Suggest three simple ways to remind myself, including one that doesn't use my phone."*

Then pick one and set it up today.
Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 19 · Read the label
Next time you're holding a food packet (or right now, from your cupboard), snap the nutrition label and ask:

*"Is this a good everyday choice for someone who's watching [salt / sugar / cholesterol]? What's one similar swap that might be better?"*

Treat it as a guide, not a rule. The best diet is still the one you and your doctor agree on.
Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 20 · Stay connected
Feeling connected is one of the best things for health at any age. Today, use AI to reach out:

*"Help me write a warm, short message to [an old friend / a grandchild / a neighbour] I haven't talked to in a while. Suggest one thing we could do together."*

Then send it. That part's up to you.
Reply DONE and tell us who you reached out to.

### Day 21 · Three weeks
Three weeks, {name}. You now know more about using AI day to day than most people. Try this one:

*"Based on what I've told you about my goals, make me a simple one-page 'my healthy week' plan with meals, movement and rest. Keep it gentle and realistic."*

Print it or pin it somewhere you'll see it.
Reply with how you're feeling about the club so far. Honest answers help us most.

## Week 4: Sharp & safe

### Day 22 · Spot the AI scam
Sadly, scammers use AI too: fake voices of loved ones, too-good-to-be-true "cures", urgent messages from "your bank". Today, ask:

*"What are the most common AI-powered scams aimed at people my age, and what are three simple rules to protect myself?"*

**A family code word is a great idea:** a secret word you'd ask for if "your grandson" ever calls needing money.
Reply DONE, or tell us if you've seen a scam lately.

### Day 23 · Is that headline true?
"This one food cures arthritis!" Headlines love a miracle. Pick a health headline you've seen recently and ask:

*"I saw a headline that says '[headline]'. What does the evidence actually say? Is it from a trustworthy source? What should I ask my doctor about it?"*

Remember Day 4: ask where to check, and how sure it is.
Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 24 · Keep your mind busy
Learning new things is good for the brain. Today, have some fun:

*"Teach me something new in five minutes: [a language phrase / a bit of history / how bread rises]. Then quiz me with three questions."*

Many members make this a morning ritual with their coffee.
Reply with the most interesting thing you learned.

### Day 25 · Your health at a glance
Let's make a one-page "about my health" sheet. It's great to have ready for appointments or emergencies. Keep it **on paper or in your phone's notes**, not in the AI chat:

*"Make me a blank one-page health summary template with sections for: my conditions, medications, allergies, doctors' phone numbers, and emergency contacts."*

Fill it in by hand. Give a copy to someone you trust.
Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 26 · Plan ahead for your checkups
Staying on top of routine checkups is one of the best things you can do. Ask:

*"What routine health checkups and screenings are commonly recommended for a [woman/man] in their [age range]? Make it a checklist I can take to my doctor."*

Your doctor will tell you which apply to you. This just helps you ask.
Reply DONE or HELP.

### Day 27 · When to call, when to wait
It helps to know which worries need a call today and which can wait. Ask:

*"In general, what kinds of symptoms mean I should call my doctor the same day, and which usually mean go to urgent care or call 911? Keep it simple."*

**If something feels seriously wrong, don't ask AI. Call 911 or your doctor.** This is just for peace of mind in advance.
Reply DONE.

### Day 28 · Teach someone else
The best way to keep a skill is to share it. Today, show a friend, partner or grandchild one thing you've learned, maybe the fridge dinner trick or the appointment list.

Or ask AI: *"Help me explain to my friend, simply, how I use you for my health."*
Reply with who you showed. That makes you a Good Rally ambassador.

Try it now for this week.
