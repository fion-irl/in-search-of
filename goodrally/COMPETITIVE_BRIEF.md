# Good Rally: context brief for competitive research

Paste this into a new Claude conversation, together with the research request at the bottom.

---

## The company
- **Name:** Good Rally (goodrally.club)
- **Mission:** AI literacy for better health.
- **Stage:** Pre-launch. Landing page and full content are built; a founding cohort is about to be recruited. No customers or revenue yet.
- **Founder:** writes *the perennial report* (perennialgirl.substack.com), a Substack about moving well, recovering well and living well for longer (movement, mobility, recovery, women's health, longevity). It has free and paid subscribers. Good Rally is presented as "from the perennial report".

## The first product: a 30-day club
- **Promise:** "Get better at your health, with AI." One short message a day for 30 days. Each one shows a simple, practical way to use a general AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, free versions) for everyday health tasks. About 5 minutes a day. No tech skills needed.
- **Delivery:** the member chooses text message, email or WhatsApp. Each message gives the exact words to type or say, and ends with "Reply DONE or HELP". A real person answers replies.
- **Curriculum:**
  - **Week 1, getting comfortable:** setting up an app, using voice mode, plain-English explanations, how to double-check AI, what never to share.
  - **Week 2, your care, organised:** appointment prep, a medication list, insurance letters and bills, after-visit summaries, symptom diary, caring for someone else.
  - **Week 3, everyday habits:** meals from a fridge photo, gentle movement for your body, sleep and wind-down, reminders, food labels, staying connected.
  - **Week 4, sharp and safe:** AI-powered scams (family code word), checking health headlines, a one-page health profile, checkup lists, when to call a doctor or 911, teaching someone else.
  - **Days 29–30:** a reusable weekly health check-in prompt, which is the lasting habit.
- **Guardrails:** "AI is your helper, never your doctor" appears every day. No diagnosis, never changing medication, never asking members for records, and teaching what not to share with AI.

## Audience
- **Primary:** adults 55–75+ who are curious about AI but haven't found a reason to use it. Not "tech people".
- **Secondary:** adult children (roughly 35–55) signing up a parent, or doing it together. The sign-up form has a "signing up for a parent" option.
- **Also:** the founder's Substack readers (midlife, health-engaged, many women).

## Business model (planned)
- **Founding cohort:** $29 for the 30 days, paid on goodrally.club via Stripe, with a full refund by day 7.
- **Paid subscribers of the perennial report** get Good Rally included (100%-off code). The Substack itself stays free.
- **Possible next steps:** a weekly membership (~$9/month) after day 30; sponsored or B2B programmes for libraries, senior centres, health systems (patient education), employers, insurers and ageing-focused nonprofits.

## Positioning, as the founder sees it (to be tested)
- **Habit, not a course:** one tiny daily action by text, not videos or a course platform.
- **Health-specific,** not general "learn AI".
- **Built for older adults:** big type, plain language, voice-first, text or WhatsApp delivery, a human who replies.
- **Literacy built in:** every day teaches a practical use *and* a judgment habit (double-check, privacy, when to call a professional).
- **Distribution advantage:** an existing health audience via Substack.
- **Known risks:**
  - AI companies may build free onboarding into their own apps.
  - Older adults may pay slowly for learning.
  - Keeping people engaged past the first week.
  - AI shopping and health agents are changing quickly.
  - "AI + health" carries trust and safety sensitivity.

## Research request

Research the competitive landscape for Good Rally. Please cover:

1. **Direct competitors:** products teaching older adults (or the general public) to use AI, especially for health. Include courses, text or email challenges, apps, newsletters and coaching programmes.
2. **Adjacent players:**
   - Digital-literacy programmes for seniors (e.g. nonprofits, library programmes, AARP-style offerings, Senior Planet-type organisations).
   - Health-literacy and patient-education tools.
   - Habit and coaching products delivered by SMS or email.
   - AI companies' own consumer onboarding and education.
   - Health systems or insurers offering AI guidance to patients.
3. **For each competitor:** what they offer, who it's for, format and delivery, price and business model, apparent traction (users, funding, partnerships), strengths, weaknesses, and how directly they overlap with Good Rally.
4. **Gaps and white space:** where Good Rally's combination (health-specific + older adults + daily text habit + literacy guardrails + creator distribution) is or isn't differentiated.
5. **Threats:** what could make Good Rally unnecessary within 1–2 years, and how likely that is.
6. **Pricing benchmarks:** what people pay for comparable consumer programmes, and what organisations pay for sponsored or B2B digital-literacy programmes.
7. **Recommendations:**
   - The 3 most important positioning decisions.
   - Partners worth approaching first.
   - Anything that should change about the product or price before launch.

Please cite sources with links, say when information is uncertain or out of date, and finish with a one-page summary table of the top 10 competitors.
